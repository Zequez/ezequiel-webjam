About Me

I was born

...on 14 of December of 1992, under the influence of Aquarius on the horizon.

My mother was to be Mirta Liliana Cappelluti, and my father Guillermo Daniel Schwartzman. Of italian and jewish-polish heritage. I was their second son.

I was delivered in the Colón Clinic, in the city of Mar del Plata, Argentina. City where I would spend most of my child and adult life.

I grew up in the neighbourhood of Punta Mogotes.

For the most part I was a sensitive child. And for the most part my early memories are a blank. I remember liking animals.

Spiritual Divorce

My earlier spiritual instruction was given by my mother, from her involvement in Escuela Científica Basilio. My father for the most part had no other interest than his business.

At school I was different than most kids my age; small and underdeveloped. Unwilling to fully participate in the human courting games, my interests shifted into computers and the Internet. I would come to study biology and physics, and under the tutelage of partriarchal education, I would become materialist. I was angry with how my body was; and so God was resonably to blame for the body I had. I found out Santa Claus was not real and so it was not such a stretch to extend that category to God. After all, science seemed that it could explain everything. And God was doing a lousy job with all those wars, poverty and hunger going on. So I became fertile ground for scientistic atheist Internet culture. I bought the cultural story; technology would save humanity; not God.

The future was awesome; there were robots and flying cars; there were automated farms and no one had to work. All energy was to be renewable; and we would surely discover fusion power in no time. Life would be extended indefinitely as we uploaded our consciousness to computers.

And so I lived on that story for some time.

Hacker Culture

As my attention shifted more and more to computers and the Internet, I took on programming as a hobby. I will never forget the ecstatic feeling of writing a prompt-based program that would make a computer do things. The power, the possibilities. Soon I learned how to code for the web, and how to publish websites, and how to write JavaScript and PHP. I would spend hours in forums reading and asking questions.

At the age of 14 I was introduced by my informatics teacher at school to the Free Software Movement. Being an atheist in a catholic school, I took refuge in the IT room that I had free pass to enter. I learned about Linux, how to format a computer with an operative system, I learned about Firefox, about Tux, about Ubuntu. I attended a talk from Richard Stallman that happened at Mar del Plata; the IT teacher took the group of geeks from school.

The truth was; copying software had no cost; and so it artificial scarcity in service of greed was easy to spot. The world would be better if we could just shared every software freely with everybody else; that was how it was meant to be. Copyright held no status or respect.

At the time I was starting to get exposed to overnight riches stories of Facebook and Microsoft. Of common folk who made lots of money on the Internet. Studying was for idiots, I would create a popular website and I would be set for life. Dropping out of university was the actual goal. Finishing university meant failure had happened, mediocrity.

And so I set out to create web apps and earn my riches. I would find a loophole to earn money without working, so I could be free and free my family too.

Tech Worker

While I created my magnum opus I had to eat, and I wanted to experience the independent life from my parents. So the skills from my web development practice gave fruits, just not the ones I was expecting. Companies started offering me jobs, and I found myself jumping between them. Working remote, earning in dollars, and traveling abroad for onboardings.

With the regular inflow of money, life got interesting quite fast. I would get to buy all the tech I wanted and visit the US and Europe traveling abroad with tech money. I got the gamer computer, the laptop, the VR headset, the robot vacuum cleaner, the high tech mattress, the 4K TV, the speakers, the smartphones, the tablets, the 3D printer and the car. I got to fulfill all my tech fantasies.

Social life was something else. I had not been in relationship with anybody. The friends I had were all like me. Tech enthusiasts and nerds. Interests were gadgets, videogames, movies, series, anime and recreational drugs.

Life got incresingly frustrated soon. Yes, I was being paid, but I was not being paid for my genius inventions. I was just doing what I was told. This was not OK.

Spiritual Renaissance

In a struggle to fighting my addiction to technology, I found refuge in Buddhism. I got introduced to mindfulness and meditation reading a book about addictions. The word procrastination was in auge in my mind at the time, and I was the worse kind. There seemed to be something wrong with me. Why would I want something so much, and not do anything about it?

Buddhist lead to binge-hearing Philosophize This podcasts, and finally I reached the end of Modern Culture. Someone had made a book named Materialism is Balooneys; and someone mentioned this book on a video from the Institute of Noetic Sciences.

A few books down and I was no longer an atheist. I could conceive of a benevolent universal intelligence at work in reality. Soon I was experimenting with psychedelics and wanted to try all the drugs in the world.

I became vegan and dropped baggage. I stopped working the souless job and did a lousy attempt at van-dwelling. I ended up without money, and selling all my tech toys. Kept my laptop and nothing else.

As I lived at my grandmother house for a time, I got really into veganism and vegan activism. Sitting in a circle next to a slaughterhouse in the outskirts of the city I met Celeste, the future mother of my son.

The purpose of my life soon was that of service to humanity. I had to awaken people. I had to help saving the world. Working for myself was beneath me. Working on something unaligned was out of the question; it was excruciatingly painful.

As I shifted my purpose, I still got jobs. The jobs I got became more holistic. First freelancing. Then a DAO. Then Holochain. It was still not enough, it was still someone else telling me what to build. I was unsatisfied.

Then everything changed

Coral was born. And my partnership with Celeste started to show it's signs of toxicity. Violent exchanges became evermore common. Working from home, an inability to set boundaries, and raising a child with someone who also was home all the time; lead to some unpleasantness.

My attention and interest shifted again. Suddenly, relating became a thing I could study. I wondered; how do I love people properly? How come all we want is to love each other and it ends so bad? Looking for ecovillages to move into; I stumbled into Possibility Management.

I soon signed up for Rage Club and was set on geting my ass into an Expand The Box. I gorged my brain in the Startover websites. I would learn more and more about conscious feelings, about low drama, about gremlin, about radical relating, about possibility teams, about holding space, about emotional healing processes, about coaching, about real magic, about initiation and about adulthood.

The desperate attempt to relate responsibly with Celeste did not end as I expected, and soon we were going our separate ways; Coral stuck in the middle of two adult-looking humans unable to relate in a healthy way with one another.

Parenting and villaging

Soon it was obvious that raising children was not something to be attempted by uninitiated grown up adolescents with a lone wolf mentality living in a city and struggling to earn money to buy food. At least not without some serious consequences.

I was set on moving into a community. Children should be able to be outside with other children in a safe place where there are no cars; not the city. Child raising was meant to be done as a village; not as nuclear families.

I didn't know how it would happen, but I had my north star. There were other people with my same values out there, and I needed to figure out how to connect with them. And the clock was ticking, Coral was growing and missing out on growing up in a healthy community.

My childhood home left to my mother, my brother and I after the divorce, became available just in time. I moved into what's now Casa Nami and called the most value-resonant friend I had to move in with me. The house featured 6 bedrooms; and he had 3 children and was also a lone father. He had also lost his house in a fire earlier that year, so it was kind of a perfect match.

Rodrigo took over the house essentially; with his entrepreneurial experience and can-do-it attitude the energy started moving. Soon there were other house dwellers. And on that glorious summer of 2026 my future partner would move into the house. We organized festivals, workshops and anything with the word holistic in it.

This was all not without it's caveat. Relating was not smooth. Spouts of violence and boundaries overstepped were common. Sharing a house, sharing a kitchen with other people was not easy. There were blockages to creating the intimacy we needed to related smoothly. Blockages that I was not absurdly proficient at overcoming, none of us were. And neither was absurdly effective at earning money either; we were getting by, convinced that we should in theory be able to work together to do something big and profitable.

Casa Nami took more dwellers; and left some; for some time there were only me and just women; that was peaceful. When another man entered the picture it seemed the flow would be altered. At Casa Nami I'm learning about women, and I'm learning about men. I'm learning how we relate with each other, what works and what does not, and what it takes to be a responsible adult.
